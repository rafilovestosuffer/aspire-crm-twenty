3.1 First login

Sign in with the credentials above. On the very first login the CRM asks for a display name and then offers to invite your team.

Watch out — the step that traps automation

"Invite your team" is a real page at /invite-team, not a dismissible pop-up, and the application's router will bounce you straight back to it if you try to navigate anywhere else. Click Skip. Any script that drives this interface has to click that link too; navigating past it does not work.

3.2 The CRM

Click any company to open it. A record page shows its own fields at the left and everything related to it down the right — the people who work there, the opportunities, and any of the custom objects that point at it.

The data model, from the inside

Open Settings → Data model. This is the provisioning step, seen from the interface.

Objects worth opening now

Consent Records is the compliance backbone. One row per person, per channel, with a status, a source and a proof string.

Automation Runs is the execution history. Every workflow writes one on every run, success or failure. This is the thing the previous platform could not provide at all, and it is one HTTP step per workflow to populate.

3.5 The other half of the demonstration

Anyone can show a system doing something. Showing it refusing to do something is what convinces an audience that the controls are real.

Submit the form again with the same email address — but this time imagine that person had previously unsubscribed. The seeded data already contains opted-out contacts; pick one from Consent Records where the status is OPTED_OUT and submit the form using that address.

The person record updates. No email is sent. And a row appears in Message Logs:

FAILED  Blocked: consent_PENDING

The tick box on the form is a request to be contacted, not an override of a previous refusal. If somebody has ever opted out, bounced or complained, a new form submission puts them into PENDING and a human has to confirm before anything is sent to them again.

Why this is the most important thing in the demonstration

A consent record that nothing consults provides no protection and considerable false assurance, which is worse than having none. Here the check is structural: there is exactly one workflow permitted to talk to the mail server, and it refuses before it sends. §4.3.
